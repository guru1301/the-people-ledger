EXECUTIVE INVESTIGATIVE THESIS
"Statewide vote share and Assembly seat share answer fundamentally different questions. Vote share measures how ballots were distributed across Tamil Nadu; seat share measures how many individual constituency contests a party won. In 2026, the distance between those two measures defined the legislative balance of power."
SECTION 1 · VOTE SHARE VS. SEAT SHARE

Elections in First-Past-The-Post parliamentary democracies are rarely determined by raw seat totals alone; rather, legislative power is governed by how efficiently political parties convert popular votes into constituency representation. In the 2026 Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly election, twelve distinct political parties secured seats in the 234-member house, but the mechanics of their conversion rates reveal dramatic disparities across the political spectrum.

An exhaustive examination of verified Google BigQuery datasets (fact_results_2026 and fact_margins_2026) demonstrates that evaluating a party's mandate strictly through its final seat count offers an incomplete picture of democratic alignment. Under First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) voting mechanics, geographic concentration, coalition dynamics, and multi-cornered vote splitting introduce non-linearities between popular support and legislative authority.

SECTION 2 · SEAT CONVERSION GAP

TVK's advantage stemmed from how its votes were converted into constituency victories. With 35.07% of valid candidate votes, TVK captured 46.15% of Assembly constituencies—a positive seat-vote conversion bonus of +11.08 percentage points.

The contrast is visible when the calculation is applied across major party platforms. DMK's seat share (25.21%) closely matched its vote share (24.28%), while AIADMK, Congress, PMK, and BJP received smaller shares of seats than their statewide popular vote shares.

SECTION 3 · PLURALITY WINS & SUB-50% VICTORIES

Winning a constituency does not require a candidate to receive more than half of all votes when several candidates divide the electorate. The candidate with the highest vote total wins the seat.

In TVK's case, this distinction was central to its electoral performance. Of its 108 constituency victories, 96 (88.89%) were secured with less than 50% of valid candidate votes. Only 12 TVK winners crossed the 50% threshold.

SECTION 4 · THE 45 COUNTERFACTUAL CONSTITUENCIES

One way to measure opposition fragmentation is to compare the winning TVK vote total with the arithmetic sum of votes received separately by DMK and AIADMK.

Across TVK's 108 victories, this combined arithmetic total exceeded the TVK winner's vote count in 45 constituencies (41.67% of all TVK wins).

METHODOLOGICAL DISCLAIMER "DMK and AIADMK contested as separate party platforms and are historic political rivals. Adding their vote totals is a mathematical counterfactual, not evidence of a political alliance and not proof that a combined candidate would have won. Voters behave dynamically when alliances or campaign conditions change."
