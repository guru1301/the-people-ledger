SECTION 1: Constitutional Framework Articles 330 & 332 • Delimitation & Joint Electorate System

Under Article 332 of the Constitution of India, Assembly constituencies are reserved for Scheduled Castes (SC) and Scheduled Tribes (ST) in proportion to their population within the state. Tamil Nadu’s 234-member Legislative Assembly features 44 SC-reserved constituencies and 2 ST-reserved constituencies, totaling 46 reserved seats (19.66% of the house).

Crucially, India operates under a Joint Electorate System established by Article 325. While nomination of candidates in SC/ST reserved seats is restricted exclusively to members of those designated communities, the entire registered electorate within the geographic boundaries of the constituency votes without demographic distinction.

SECTION 4: Electoral Competitiveness & Victory Margins Comparing SC Reserved vs General Victory Margins

Statistical evaluation of the 2026 certified electoral returns reveals a significant competitiveness differential between SC-reserved and General constituencies. Scheduled Caste reserved seats recorded tighter average winning margins (14,192 votes) compared to General seats (17,544 votes)—representing a 19.1% narrower victory margin (-3,352 votes).

Median victory margins confirm this trend: SC reserved seats recorded a median margin of 11,840 votes, whereas General seats registered a median margin of 14,620 votes. This tighter spread indicates higher voter contestation and elevated political parity in reserved seats.

SECTION 9: Comprehensive Investigative Report Constitutional Mandate, Competitiveness & Electoral Outcomes in Reserved Seats

The constitutional design of India's electoral democracy incorporates affirmative representation through Articles 330 and 332, reserving legislative seats for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in proportion to their population. In Tamil Nadu, 46 of the 234 Assembly seats are reserved—44 for Scheduled Castes and 2 for Scheduled Tribes. Under the Joint Electorate system established by Article 325, while candidacy in reserved seats is restricted to SC/ST community members, all electors within the constituency participate equally in choosing their representative.

Analysis of the 2026 Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly election returns reveals distinct electoral patterns across reserved constituencies. SC-reserved seats exhibited higher electoral competitiveness than General seats. The average winning margin in SC-reserved constituencies was 14,192 votes, compared to 17,544 votes in General constituencies—a difference of 3,352 votes (19.1% narrower). The median winning margin also reflected this tighter distribution, recording 11,840 votes in SC seats versus 14,620 votes in General seats.

Party performance across the 44 SC-reserved seats showed significant polarization. Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam (TVK) won 23 SC-reserved seats, representing 52.3% of all SC seats. The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK) secured 9 SC seats (20.5%), while the All India Anna Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (AIADMK) won 8 SC seats (18.2%). The Viduthalai Chiruthaigal Katchi (VCK) won 2 SC seats (4.5%), while the Communist Party of India (CPI) and CPI (Marxist) won 1 SC seat each (2.3%).

Comparing TVK's performance across reservation categories demonstrates a higher win rate in SC constituencies. TVK contested all 234 seats, winning 84 of 188 General seats (44.7%), 23 of 44 SC seats (52.3%), and 1 of 2 ST seats (50.0%). The 7.6 percentage point increase in TVK's win rate in SC seats compared to General seats indicates strong candidate contestation in reserved areas.

The state's 2 ST-reserved constituencies—Yercaud (AC 83) in Salem district and Senthamangalam (AC 93) in Namakkal district—were divided between TVK and AIADMK. TVK won Yercaud by a margin of 12,410 votes (5.4%), while AIADMK won Senthamangalam by 14,820 votes (6.8%). Given the small sample size of two seats, broader conclusions regarding ST voter preferences require caution.

In conclusion, reserved constituencies played a central role in shaping the political landscape of the 2026 Tamil Nadu Assembly. SC-reserved seats demonstrated closer electoral contests than General seats while delivering a majority of their mandates to TVK, alongside competitive representation across DMK, AIADMK, VCK, and Left parties.

FINAL CONCLUSION

Reserved constituencies played a significant role in shaping the 2026 Tamil Nadu Assembly. Representing 46 of the state's 234 Assembly seats, these constituencies exhibited two distinct characteristics. First, Scheduled Caste constituencies recorded lower average and median victory margins than General constituencies, indicating relatively closer electoral contests. Second, the political outcome differed across these seats, with Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam winning 23 of the 44 Scheduled Caste constituencies while the two Scheduled Tribe constituencies were evenly divided between TVK and AIADMK. Together, these findings provide a comprehensive picture of competition, constitutional representation and party performance across Tamil Nadu's reserved constituencies.
