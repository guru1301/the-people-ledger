SECTION 2 · UNDERSTANDING ELECTION DEPOSITS Legal framework & deterrent mechanics

Under Section 34 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, every candidate contesting an Assembly election in India must deposit a financial security sum with the Election Commission of India (ECI) — currently set at ₹10,000 for General category candidates and ₹5,000 for Scheduled Castes (SC) and Scheduled Tribes (ST) candidates.

This security deposit is returned only if the candidate secures more than one-sixth (16.67%) of the total valid votes polled in their constituency. If a candidate fails to cross this 16.67% threshold, the deposit is forfeited to the state treasury.

The primary objective of this legal mechanism is to deter non-serious candidacies from overcrowding ballot papers and electronic voting machines, thereby preserving the focus of democratic choices.

SECTION 3 · STATEWIDE INVESTIGATION Electoral concentration vs hyper-fragmentation

The 2026 Tamil Nadu Assembly elections witnessed 4,023 candidates contesting across 234 assembly divisions — an average of 17 candidates per seat. However, beneath this apparent multitude of choices lies an extraordinary structural reality: 3,352 candidates (83.32%) failed to retain their security deposits.

This means that for more than eight out of every ten candidates who stood on the ballot, these candidates did not secure enough valid votes to retain their election deposits under statutory rules.

Data analysis reveals that voters overwhelmingly concentrated their ballots onto top contenders in each constituency, leaving independent candidates, minor local factions, and registered unrecognized parties with micro-shares ranging from 0.05% to 2.10%.

SECTION 4 · HOW COMPETITIVE WAS TAMIL NADU? Vote concentration among top contenders

While the presence of 17 candidates on an average ballot paper gives the visual impression of multi-party competition, electoral data demonstrates that vote shares were heavily concentrated among top contenders. Across the state's 234 assembly divisions, voters overwhelmingly concentrated their support on leading candidates, leaving the vast majority of contesting candidates below the 16.67% threshold.

In most assembly seats, only candidates fielded by major political alliances managed to cross the deposit retention threshold, while long-tail independent candidates polled single-digit or fractional vote percentages.

SECTION 6 · DISTRICT INVESTIGATION Regional variations in deposit loss rates

District-level analysis reveals significant regional variation in deposit forfeitures across Tamil Nadu's 38 administrative districts.

Karur (92.77% Forfeiture Rate — Highest Rate): Karur recorded the highest forfeiture rate in the state (92.77%), driven largely by the unusually high number of candidates contesting the Karur Assembly Constituency (154 out of 166 candidates forfeited deposits).

Chennai (90.97% Forfeiture Rate — Largest Volume): Chennai, meanwhile, recorded the largest absolute number of forfeitures (443 candidates) because it had the highest number of contestants (487 candidates) across its 16 constituencies.

The Nilgiris (57.14% Forfeiture Rate — Lowest Rate): At the other end of the spectrum, The Nilgiris recorded the lowest forfeiture rate (57.14%), indicating a comparatively smaller candidate field (21 candidates across 3 constituencies) and a higher proportion of candidates (9 candidates or 42.86%) crossing the statutory threshold.

SECTION 8 · INVESTIGATIVE FEATURE ARTICLE The Price of Contesting — Special Longform Report

An Election Crowded With Candidates

When nominations closed for the 2026 Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly election, official figures listed 4,023 candidates across 234 divisions. On paper, it was one of the largest fields of contenders in the state's election history. Yet, as vote counting concluded, official returns exposed a striking reality: 3,352 of those candidates failed to secure even one-sixth of the valid votes cast in their respective constituencies, resulting in the forfeiture of their statutory security deposits.

When Competition Becomes Fragmentation

The phenomenon of candidate inflation has long been a feature of Indian elections. Proponents argue that high candidate participation reflects democratic enthusiasm and low barriers to entry. However, data from the 2026 elections indicates that candidate crowding primarily creates ballot paper fragmentation.

In constituencies like Karur—where 79 candidates appeared on the ballot—the vast majority of contenders polled fewer than 300 votes each. Most independent candidates received only a small share of the valid votes, indicating limited electoral support relative to established political party machinery.

The Cost of Being an Independent Candidate

Under Election Commission rules, the security deposit serves as a financial threshold. For independent candidates and unorganized parties, forfeiture means failing to reach the 16.67% vote benchmark. Statewide, 2,201 independent candidates forfeited their security deposits, polling a collective average of less than 0.5% per candidate.

Why Chennai Produced Hundreds of Deposit Forfeitures

Metropolitan Chennai logged 427 forfeited deposits out of 471 candidates (90.66%)—the largest absolute number of forfeitures in Tamil Nadu across its 16 constituencies. While Karur recorded the state's highest forfeiture rate (92.77%), Chennai registered high candidate filing numbers, leaving hundreds of unorganized contenders below the statutory deposit threshold.

Who Actually Managed To Save Their Deposits?

Deposit retention was primarily achieved by nominees of recognized political coalitions. The DMK-led alliance saved 232 out of 234 deposits (99.15%), TVK saved 230 out of 234 deposits (98.29%), and the AIADMK coalition saved 228 out of 234 deposits (97.44%). Only 8 out of 2,209 independent candidates statewide managed to cross the 16.67% vote mark.

Beyond Winning: The Hidden Story Of Electoral Survival

Ultimately, Finding #4 demonstrates that while anyone can pay the fee to place their name on a Tamil Nadu ballot paper, surviving the 16.67% electoral threshold requires substantial voter support. For 83.32% of candidates, contesting the election resulted in falling below this statutory benchmark.

SECTION 10 · CONCLUSION Final assessment on democratic participation

Finding #4 reveals a fundamental characteristic of Tamil Nadu's elections: while nominations remain accessible to all eligible citizens, voter choices are concentrated among leading contenders. The 83.32% deposit forfeiture rate demonstrates that the vast majority of candidates fail to secure the 16.67% vote share required to retain their security deposits.

"In the final ledger of Tamil Nadu's 2026 election, democracy permitted 4,023 voices onto the ballot—but voters required eight out of ten to pay the price of admission."
