1. THE STATEWIDE FINDING

A rigorous mathematical analysis of registered electors and votes cast reveals a clear participation gap in Tamil Nadu's 2026 Assembly election. Female electors recorded a higher turnout rate than male electors when votes cast were measured against their respective registered elector populations. The weighted statewide calculation places female turnout at 86.20%, against 83.77% for male electors—a difference of +2.42 percentage points.

But a statewide average can conceal substantial local variation. To determine whether this advantage was widespread or driven by a small number of constituencies, The People's Ledger examined the gender turnout gap across all 234 Assembly constituencies.

METHODOLOGY & SCOPE

Female Turnout (%) = Female_Voted / Female_Electors × 100
Male Turnout (%) = Male_Voted / Male_Electors × 100
Gender Gap = Female Turnout − Male Turnout

This analysis measures participation rates using official elector rolls. It does not measure voter preference, party alignment, or candidate choice.

2. THE PATTERN WAS BROAD, BUT NOT UNIVERSAL

The statewide advantage was not confined to a handful of constituencies with exceptionally large gaps. Female turnout exceeded male turnout in 169 of 234 assembly constituencies (72.2% of all Assembly seats). Male turnout was higher in 65 constituencies (27.8%), while 0 recorded equal rates.

The result therefore describes a broad statewide tendency rather than a uniform rule: higher female participation was more common across Tamil Nadu, but a substantial minority of constituencies moved in the opposite direction.

3. HOW LARGE WAS THE ADVANTAGE?

Counting the number of female-leading constituencies shows how widespread the pattern was; grouping the gaps by size reveals its intensity.

The largest single group consisted of constituencies where the female advantage was relatively narrow, between zero and two percentage points. At the other end of the distribution, a smaller group recorded female advantages of ten percentage points or more.

NOTE ON SCALE
The evidence shows two things at once: female turnout led in a clear majority of constituencies, but the size of that lead varied substantially across the state.
4. THE STATEWIDE AVERAGE HIDES MUCH WIDER LOCAL GAPS

The statewide turnout difference provides an overall measure, but individual constituencies reveal considerably wider disparities. At the top of the ranking, Tiruvadanai recorded the state's largest female turnout advantage at +13.82 percentage points, with female turnout of 83.66% compared with 69.83% among male electors.

5. COUNTER-EVIDENCE: PATTERN REVERSED IN 65 CONSTITUENCIES

The statewide female advantage did not extend to every constituency. Male turnout exceeded female turnout in 65 seats, accounting for 27.8% of the state.

The strongest reversal occurred in Kancheepuram, where male turnout exceeded female turnout by 2.78 percentage points. The counter-evidence demonstrates that the statewide finding should not be interpreted as a universal constituency-level outcome.

6. DISTRICT-LEVEL EVIDENCE

The constituency analysis can be tested at a broader geographic level by aggregating raw elector and voter counts within each district. Using this weighted method, female turnout remained higher in 30 of 38 districts, while male turnout led in 8 of 38 districts.

The strongest district-level female advantage was recorded in Ramanathapuram at +11.50 percentage points. At the opposite end, Kancheepuram recorded the strongest male advantage at -2.15 points.

The district results reinforce the broader statewide finding while also identifying areas where the direction of the turnout gap reversed.

7. TWO SCALES, SAME QUESTION

At the statewide level, female turnout exceeded male turnout. At the constituency level, the same direction appeared in 169 of 234 seats. At the district level, it appeared in 30 of 38 districts.

Together, these measurements show that the female turnout advantage was visible at statewide, constituency, and district levels.

8. WHAT THE NUMBERS ESTABLISH

The evidence establishes a clear participation pattern in Tamil Nadu's 2026 Assembly election. Female electors recorded a higher weighted turnout rate statewide, and that advantage appeared across a majority of constituencies and districts.

Yet the size and even the direction of the gap varied geographically. The strongest female-leading constituency recorded a double-digit advantage, while dozens of constituencies recorded male turnout leads.

The central finding is therefore not that every part of Tamil Nadu behaved identically. It is that higher female turnout was the more common pattern across the state's electoral landscape.
