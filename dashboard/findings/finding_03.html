1. The Scale of Rejection Statewide NOTA Summary

Across all 234 assembly constituencies in the 2026 Tamil Nadu elections, a total of 199,801 voters pressed the NOTA button — choosing to formally reject every candidate on the ballot. This represented 0.41% of the 49,324,119 total votes cast (including NOTA).

The mean constituency-level NOTA rate was 0.41%. The highest NOTA rate was recorded in Udhagamandalam (The Nilgiris) at 1.03%, while the lowest was Mailam (Viluppuram) at just 0.16%.

While NOTA's absolute share appears small, its significance emerges when measured against victory margins. Under India's First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) system, even a single-vote margin is fully decisive. NOTA does not transfer to any candidate, nor does it invalidate a result. It is a metric of voter rejection — not an electoral reset mechanism.

2. The 11-Seat Margin Investigation Constituencies Where NOTA Votes Exceeded the Victory Margin

In 11 of 234 constituencies (4.70%), the number of NOTA votes was larger than the margin separating the winner from the runner-up. This does not mean NOTA changed any result. Under FPTP rules, a 1-vote lead is as decisive as a 100,000-vote lead. What these numbers reveal is the scale of visible voter dissatisfaction relative to the closeness of the contest.

Seenivasa Sethupathy R. (TVK) won with 83,375 votes against Periakaruppan KR (DMK) who received 83,374 votes — a margin of just 1 vote. Meanwhile, 747 voters in the same constituency chose NOTA. The rejected-ballot count was 746 times larger than the margin of victory — the most extreme ratio in the state.

This does not mean NOTA altered the outcome. Under FPTP, the single-vote lead is fully valid and legally decisive. But the contrast between a 1-vote margin and 747 dissatisfied voters illustrates the gap between electoral mechanics and voter sentiment.

4. Cross-Party Evidence Winner Parties in NOTA > Margin Seats

The 11 constituencies where NOTA exceeded the victory margin were not monopolized by any single party. Multiple parties held winning positions in these tight seats, confirming that NOTA-sensitive margins are distributed across the political spectrum — not concentrated against one alliance.

Analytical Limitations & Methodological Note

"This investigation does not claim that NOTA changed, altered, or influenced any election result. Under India's First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) system, even a single-vote margin is fully decisive and legally valid. NOTA votes do not transfer to any candidate, nor do they trigger a re-election."

"The comparison between NOTA votes and victory margins is a mathematical observation — not a causal claim. Voter motivations for choosing NOTA are unobservable from aggregate data alone."

"NOTA rates in this analysis use total votes cast (including NOTA) as the denominator, consistent with ECI reporting standards."
